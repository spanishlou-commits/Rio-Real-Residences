TL;DR: The Short Version

Everything you need to know about Rio Real in two minutes

Rio Real is a residential area on the eastern edge of Marbella, quiet, established and beautifully positioned between the Mediterranean coastline and the wooded hills above the city. It's home to one of Marbella's finest golf courses, the celebrated Senda Litoral coastal boardwalk, excellent schools and some of the most spectacular views anywhere on the Costa del Sol. Marbella's historic centre is five minutes by car. Málaga Airport is 35 minutes. The beach is closer than you might expect. And the quality of everyday life, the sense of space, the greenery, the calm, is something that visitors notice immediately and residents never stop appreciating.

Why Rio Real

A rare balance of peace and convenience

There are more famous addresses in Marbella. There are flashier ones too. But for a certain kind of buyer, one who values quality of life over proximity to the nightlife, Rio Real consistently comes out on top.

What makes it different is hard to pin to a single thing. It's partly the green corridors and mature gardens that line the streets, partly the fact that the communities here feel established rather than newly built. It's partly the views, something you never quite take for granted, no matter how long you've lived here. And it's partly something more atmospheric: Rio Real simply feels like somewhere people actually live, rather than a resort area dressed up as one.

The international community here is well-established and genuinely diverse. Swedish families who've been here for decades sit alongside British retirees, Dutch professionals, Spanish families and a newer wave of buyers from Germany, Belgium and Scandinavia. There's a neighbourly quality that can be surprisingly rare in this part of the world, people who know each other, who've been here long enough to have opinions about schools and restaurants and the best route for an early morning walk.

And then there's the convenience. The detail that surprises most first-time visitors: Marbella's historic old town, with its restaurants, boutiques and plazas, is a five-minute drive. La Cañada, the main shopping centre for the area, is the same. The A-7 motorway, connecting you to the whole Costa del Sol, is moments away. You come home to peace and quiet. You don't have to drive an hour to reach anything worth visiting.

Location

Where exactly is Rio Real?

Rio Real occupies a privileged position on the eastern edge of Marbella, sandwiched between the Mediterranean Sea and the foothills of the Sierra Blanca. The name comes from the Río Real, the small river that once defined the eastern boundary of the town, and the area has developed organically around the golf course that was laid out along its banks in the 1960s.

From many parts of Rio Real, and particularly from the elevated communities above the main road, the views are exceptional. On clear days, which, on the Costa del Sol, is most days, you can see Marbella stretching along the coastline to the west, Gibraltar rising from the sea to the south-west and, on the clearest winter mornings, the Rif Mountains of North Africa beyond the Strait.

The area sits between the A-7 coast road and the AP-7 motorway, which means getting in and out is straightforward in every direction. East towards Estepona and Sotogrande, west towards Marbella and Puerto Banús, or north towards Ronda through the mountains, the connections are excellent.

When you can reach the airport in 35 minutes, international travel becomes less stressful. When Marbella's restaurants and old town are five minutes away, spontaneous evenings out become genuinely spontaneous rather than a logistical undertaking. And when you can walk along the coast path into the city on a Sunday morning, the weekly rhythm shifts in ways that are hard to put a value on but easy to appreciate.

"You can be in the heart of Marbella in five minutes and back home to birdsong in five more. That balance is rarer than it sounds."

For international buyers, the position on Marbella's eastern side has another practical advantage: it keeps you clear of the busiest tourist areas concentrated around Puerto Banús and the Golden Mile, while keeping you well within reach of all the amenities that make Marbella worth choosing in the first place.

The Urbanisations

The different communities of Rio Real

One thing many buyers discover only once they start looking: Rio Real isn't a single development. It's a collection of distinct residential communities, each with its own character, architecture and feel. Understanding the differences matters, the right community depends entirely on what you're looking for.

Rio Real Golf
Golf · Apartments & Villas · Established

The area that started it all. Built around the championship golf course, this is where you'll find mature gardens, manicured fairways and a mix of well-established apartments, townhouses and villas. Popular with golfers, second-home owners and buyers who want a peaceful, green setting with a genuine community feel. The golf club's hotel and restaurant add a social dimension that benefits all residents, whether they play or not.

Lomas de Pozuelo
Views · Townhouses & Villas · Local Favourite

If we had to choose one community that captures everything we love about Rio Real, it would be this one. Perched above Marbella on a hillside that catches the afternoon light perfectly, Lomas de Pozuelo enjoys some of the most extraordinary views anywhere in the area. The drive home each evening, as you crest the hill and the whole city opens up below you, the sea glittering in the distance, is something that never loses its effect. This is where we both live, and one of the reasons we fell in love with Rio Real in the first place.

Finca de Marbella
Contemporary · Private Villas · International Buyers

A modern community of contemporary villas with clean, confident architecture, private pools, generous gardens and easy access to both Marbella and the beach. Popular with international buyers and families relocating to the Costa del Sol who are looking for a contemporary home with privacy, without sacrificing location. La Finca de Marbella has attracted a diverse, international community that feels established while remaining design-forward.

The List
Boutique · Luxury · Contemporary Design

One of Marbella's more recent boutique additions to the Rio Real area, The List offers a smaller number of luxury contemporary homes with shared facilities and a level of design and finish that sets it apart. Suited to buyers who want something new, private and well-appointed, without the scale of a larger development.

Monteros Palm Beach
Frontline Beach · Apartments · Beach Lifestyle

As the name suggests, Monteros Palm Beach sits right on the coastline, frontline beach apartments within walking distance of some of Marbella's finest stretches of sand. A very different proposition from the elevated communities above: this is about waking up to the sound of the sea, stepping onto the beach in minutes and living the coastal lifestyle without compromise. Particularly popular with buyers for whom the beach is the primary consideration.

Altamira
Established · Apartments · Garden Setting

An established residential community known for its generous apartments, mature communal gardens and peaceful surroundings. Altamira attracts buyers who are looking for space, established greenery and a quiet neighbourhood without the premium price tag of some of the newer developments. A solid, well-regarded community with a long-standing population.

Torre Real
Golf & Sea Views · Apartments · Well-Positioned

Built around the coastline and the golf course, Torre Real offers spacious apartments with some excellent sea and golf views. The position makes it a popular choice for buyers who want the best of both, proximity to the sea and the fairways in a well-maintained, established setting.

Real Panorama
Elevated · Panoramic Views · Value

An elevated residential area that delivers exactly what its name promises, panoramic Mediterranean views from a hillside position that few communities at this price point can match. Real Panorama often represents excellent value compared to some neighbouring developments, making it a popular choice for buyers who want the views without the highest price tag in the area.

Las Lomas de Río Real
Nature Setting · Apartments & Penthouses · Privacy

Luxury apartments and penthouses tucked into a natural, green setting between the golf course and the hillside. Las Lomas de Río Real offers privacy and a feeling of being surrounded by nature, while remaining only minutes from both Marbella and the beach. A good option for buyers who want the space and calm of a rural setting without the isolation.

Rio Real Golf

One of Marbella's most respected courses

Golf has shaped Rio Real since the beginning. The course was designed in 1964 by Javier Arana, the same architect responsible for El Saler and El Prat, two of Spain's most celebrated courses, and it has been woven into the character of the area ever since.

The layout winds through mature pines and river vegetation before finishing close to the Mediterranean, offering views of the sea on the final holes that make for a genuinely memorable round. It isn't the most dramatic course on the Costa del Sol, it doesn't have the mountain theatre of some inland courses or the ocean frontage of the most glamorous coastal designs, but it has something arguably more valuable: integrity. It's a proper test of golf, well-maintained, with a club culture that feels established rather than manufactured.

The club's hotel and restaurant are an asset to the whole community, not just golfers. The restaurant is a popular choice for dinner among locals, and the terrace, overlooking the course and, beyond it, the sea, is one of the better places in the area to watch the sun go down. Even if you never play a round, the presence of the golf course contributes enormously to the atmosphere of Rio Real: the mature trees, the open green space, the unhurried pace of the area on a quiet morning.

  • Designed by Javier Arana, one of Spain's finest golf architects
  • 18 holes winding through pines and river landscape
  • Sea views on the closing holes
  • Hotel, restaurant and terrace open to the public
  • A social hub for the wider Rio Real community
  • Walking distance from several urbanisations
Beaches & Outdoor Living

Where the beach becomes part of everyday life

Living in Rio Real means the beach is never far away, and not just as a destination. For many residents, the coast is woven into the daily rhythm of life in a way that's one of the more quietly extraordinary aspects of living here.

The Senda Litoral

Of all the things we'd recommend about Rio Real, the Senda Litoral might be the one that surprises people most. This wooden coastal boardwalk runs for kilometres along the Mediterranean shoreline, connecting beaches, beach clubs and restaurants all the way into Marbella.

Residents walk it, cycle it, run it early in the morning before the summer heat takes hold. On a winter evening, with the light dropping low over the sea and almost no one else around, it's genuinely beautiful. The path connects Rio Real directly to Marbella's seafront, which means reaching the city on foot or by bike is not just possible, it's pleasant enough that people choose to do it rather than drive.

It's one of those infrastructure details that sounds modest but changes how you live. When the walk into town is along the Mediterranean, you walk into town differently.

The beaches

Rio Real Beach is the local beach, quiet, accessible and well-maintained. On weekday mornings, especially in the shoulder seasons, it's the kind of beach where you can swim and barely see another person.

Trocadero is one of Marbella's most celebrated dining spots, a beachfront restaurant that does long, sun-soaked lunches particularly well. The kind of place where a long lunch becomes the afternoon without anyone particularly minding.

The Point Marbella brings a younger energy, beach club, pool, jet ski hire and a summer programme of activities that makes it popular with families and groups. A different experience from the quieter stretches nearby, and easily worth a visit.

Los Monteros Beach offers another option nearby, long, sandy and reliably uncrowded by the standards of the Costa del Sol's busier resorts.

Dog Beach, Marbella's designated dog-friendly beach is a short drive east, and for dog owners in Rio Real it becomes a morning ritual. Early starts, sea air and a dog who sleeps through the afternoon. There are worse ways to begin a day.

Kitesurfing and paddleboarding: The eastern beaches around Rio Real regularly catch excellent wind conditions, making them popular with kitesurfers and paddleboarders. If you've ever wanted to learn, this is a good stretch of coast to do it.

Education

Schools near Rio Real: what local families actually say

For families considering a move to Rio Real, schools are often the first question. The good news: the area is well-served, with a genuine choice between private, international and state options, and local families speak highly of schools across all three categories.

  • Colegio Alborán
    A well-respected private school offering a high-quality Spanish education, popular with Spanish families and international buyers who want their children fully immersed in Spanish language and culture.

They have a strong reputation, and the local knowledge we can offer here is genuine, our own daughter attended Pinolivo, Colegio Platero and Las Dunas, and we'd happily recommend each of them.

Daily Life

Everything you need, within minutes

One of the underrated advantages of Rio Real is how genuinely convenient everyday life is. This isn't an area where you drive 30 minutes to pick up a prescription or spend an hour looking for a decent supermarket. The infrastructure for modern life, shopping, healthcare, sport, dining, is close, varied and good.

La Cañada Shopping Centre, five minutes away, is the main anchor, home to an Alcampo hypermarket, a good range of shops and services, and a food court. For supermarkets, Mercadona is five minutes in the other direction in Marbella town. It covers the basics efficiently. For anything more specific, Marbella's old town and the commercial areas along the A-7 fill the gaps.

Healthcare is well-provided for in this part of Marbella, with private clinics, specialist consultants and English-speaking GPs established across the area. The private hospital provision around Marbella is generally considered good by European standards, and the public hospital in Marbella, the Hospital Costa del Sol, is a modern, well-equipped facility.

Sport and fitness are woven into life here in a way that makes it easier to stay active than not. Golf is the obvious anchor, but the area also has gyms, padel clubs (padel is something of a community sport in this part of Spain), tennis clubs and easy access to cycling and running routes along the Senda Litoral and into the hills. The outdoor lifestyle on offer here is genuinely excellent.

Restaurants are more varied than the residential setting might suggest. Rio Real Golf has its own restaurant, and the beachfront options, Trocadero in particular, are destination dining for the whole of Marbella. Marbella's old town, five minutes away, has an extraordinary range of restaurants for a city of its size, from Michelin-starred kitchens to neighbourhood tapas bars that have barely changed in decades.

  • La Cañada Shopping Centre, 5 minutes (Alcampo hypermarket and more)
  • Marbella Old Town, restaurants, boutiques and the weekly market
  • Private clinics and English-speaking GPs throughout the area
  • Padel clubs and tennis centres within 10 minutes
  • Gyms and fitness studios in both Marbella East and the town centre
  • Beachfront dining at Trocadero and The Point Marbella
  • Rio Real Golf restaurant and hotel terrace
  • Public bus services connecting to Marbella centre
Who lives here

Rio Real suits many different lives

Part of what makes Rio Real genuinely interesting as a place to live is the range of people it attracts. The same area suits a family with school-age children, a retired couple who've been coming to Marbella for 20 years, a remote-working professional and an investor looking for long-term returns. That breadth of appeal is not accidental, it reflects something real about the place.

Families

Quiet residential streets, generous garden space, excellent schools across all types, and easy access to beaches and sports facilities make Rio Real consistently popular with families. The communities here feel safe, established and genuinely neighbourly. Children have space. Parents have convenience. And the beach is never more than a few minutes away.

Retirees

A remarkable combination of walkability, climate, healthcare access and lifestyle quality makes Rio Real one of the most genuinely liveable areas on the Costa del Sol for retirement. Golf on the doorstep. The Senda Litoral for daily walks. Restaurants and cafés minutes away. Málaga Airport 35 minutes for regular flights home. And a pace of life that manages to feel both sociable and calm.

Investors

Land in Rio Real is limited. The area's established character means new large-scale development is constrained. Demand from international buyers, particularly Scandinavian, British, German and Dutch, has been consistent for decades. The combination of location, lifestyle appeal and restricted supply has supported long-term values. It's not the flashiest postcode in Marbella, but it has shown durable appeal.

Holiday Home Buyers

For buyers looking for a second home that genuinely earns its keep, Rio Real offers strong rental demand from the quality end of the holiday market, excellent year-round weather, a manageable flight from most of Northern Europe and a lifestyle that makes visits feel like a genuine reset rather than just a change of scenery. Properties here tend to attract visitors looking for quality over novelty.

Insider Knowledge

Local tips from Rio Real residents

The things that don't appear on any property listing or travel website, but that matter enormously to how much you enjoy living somewhere.

Best Sunset
Lomas de Pozuelo, facing west. Park at the top of the hill before the road begins to descend. The view over Marbella as the light drops, with Gibraltar catching the last of it in the distance, is genuinely extraordinary.
Morning Walk
The Senda Litoral heading west at 7am. Before the heat, before the crowds. The sea is often glassy. It takes about 40 minutes to reach Marbella's seafront and the walk is flat the whole way.
Best Coffee
Head into Marbella's old town for the best café options, there are several excellent spots around the Plaza de los Naranjos. The drive is five minutes. Worth it every time.
Quietest Beach
Rio Real Beach on a winter morning, or any morning before 10am in summer. The beach empties out more quickly than most Marbella beaches, and in the shoulder seasons you can have a long stretch almost to yourself.
Best Lunch
Trocadero on a weekday. Weekends can be busy. A Tuesday lunch in early October, when the summer has passed and the terrace has room, it's one of the better restaurant experiences in this part of Marbella.
Best Family Activity
The Point Marbella for families with children, the combination of beach, pool, activities and lunch makes for an easy all-day outing without the need to move the car. Jet skis for older children, calm water for younger ones.
Best Dog Walk
The Senda Litoral east from Rio Real, quieter than the stretch towards Marbella and excellent for dogs. Then south to the dog beach, where they can run off-lead. A genuinely good early-morning circuit.
Hidden Gem
The viewpoint above Lomas de Pozuelo at dawn in winter. When the air is clear and the light is coming up, Gibraltar and the North African coast are both visible. Almost no one outside the neighbourhood knows about it.
Best Golf View
The terrace at Rio Real Golf Club in the late afternoon. The course catches the light beautifully at this time of day, and the sea is visible beyond the trees on the final holes. Non-golfers welcome.
Local Knowledge
Avoid the A-7 towards Marbella between 8am and 9am on weekdays during term time. Take the upper road through the urbanisations instead, 10 minutes longer but stress-free. Worth knowing from day one.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about living in Rio Real

Real questions from buyers, families and investors who've asked us about Rio Real over the years, answered honestly from local experience.

Is Rio Real a good place to live?

Yes, consistently so. Rio Real is one of Marbella's most established and respected residential areas, offering a quiet, green environment without the isolation that some rural or inland areas can bring. It attracts a broad mix of residents: families with children in local schools, retirees who've been here for decades, professionals working remotely and second-home owners who return year after year. The quality of everyday life is high, the community is genuinely mixed and the location is exceptional. Marbella's historic centre is five minutes away. The beach is closer. And the sense of coming home to somewhere peaceful and beautiful doesn't diminish with time.

Where exactly is Rio Real, Marbella?

Rio Real sits on the eastern edge of Marbella, between the Mediterranean coastline and the foothills of the Sierra Blanca. It is named after the Río Real, a small river that runs through the area, and has developed over the past 60 years around the golf course that was laid out along the river valley in the 1960s. The area is bounded by the A-7 coast road to the south and the AP-7 motorway further north, with residential communities spread across the hillsides between. From many parts of Rio Real, the views extend across Marbella, the sea and, on clear days, Gibraltar and North Africa.

Can you walk from Rio Real into Marbella?

Yes, and it's genuinely worth doing. The Senda Litoral, a wooden coastal boardwalk that follows the Mediterranean shoreline, connects Rio Real directly to Marbella's seafront. The walk takes around 40 minutes at a relaxed pace and is flat for most of its length. Many residents walk or cycle it regularly, not just for the exercise but because the route itself is one of the more pleasant things about living in this part of Marbella. The path passes beaches, beach clubs and the occasional café kiosk, and in the cooler months it's particularly good in the early morning before the rest of the world is awake.

Is Rio Real close to the beach?

Very much so. Rio Real has its own beach, quiet and well-maintained, and several of Marbella's most popular stretches of sand, including Los Monteros Beach, Trocadero and The Point Marbella, are just a few minutes away by car. Some of the lower urbanisations, particularly Monteros Palm Beach, are within a short walk of the coast. For most residents, the beach is a practical part of daily life rather than a destination requiring any particular effort to reach.

What schools are near Rio Real?

Families have a good range of options. Private and international schools in the area include English International College, one of the Costa del Sol's most established British curriculum schools, Las Chapas Bilingual School, which offers Spanish-English bilingual education, and Colegio Alborán, a private Spanish school. For state school options, the quality is better than many international buyers expect. Our own daughter attended Pinolivo, Colegio Platero and Las Dunas, and we'd recommend all three based on genuine personal experience. If you'd like detailed and honest advice about school choices in Rio Real, it's one of the things we're particularly well-placed to help with.

Is Rio Real family-friendly?

Yes, consistently so. The residential communities are quiet and well-managed, with good green space and a genuinely neighbourly atmosphere. Schools are close and varied. Beaches, sports facilities and outdoor activities are all easily accessible. The pace of life suits children, there's space, there's nature, there's the sea nearby. Families who move here tend to stay.

Which part of Rio Real has the best views?

Most local residents would say Lomas de Pozuelo, and we'd agree. Elevated above Marbella on a hillside that catches the afternoon light beautifully, Lomas de Pozuelo has sweeping panoramic views across the city, the Mediterranean and, on clear days, Gibraltar and the Moroccan coast. The drive home as the sun sets is something that genuinely never loses its effect. Real Panorama is also worth mentioning, it delivers excellent views for a slightly different price point. The highest parts of Finca de Marbella also have strong views towards the sea.

Is Rio Real good for investment?

Rio Real has shown consistent long-term appeal as a residential location, and the fundamentals that support that are unlikely to change: limited land for new development, established community character, strong international demand (particularly from Scandinavian, British, German and Dutch buyers) and proximity to Marbella without being absorbed into the busier tourist areas. It's not the flashiest postcode on the Costa del Sol, but it has shown durable value over time. For buyers looking at rental income, the area attracts the quality end of the holiday market, visitors who want space, privacy and location rather than the resort experience.

Is Rio Real only for golfers?

Not at all. While Rio Real Golf is one of Marbella's most respected courses and the golf course setting defines the landscape of parts of the area, a large proportion of residents don't play golf. People choose Rio Real for its tranquillity, its mature green surroundings, its proximity to the beach and its easy access to Marbella. Golf is part of the atmosphere, the mature trees, the open space, the unhurried pace, rather than a prerequisite. The golf club's restaurant and hotel are used regularly by non-golfing residents too.

Do I need a car to live in Rio Real?

A car makes everyday life more convenient, particularly for La Cañada shopping runs and for reaching parts of Marbella that aren't on the coastal path. That said, many residents manage well without one, or with occasional car use supplemented by walking, cycling and taxis. The Senda Litoral puts Marbella's seafront and old town within easy reach on foot or by bike. Public bus services run between Rio Real and Marbella centre. And with Uber and local taxi services readily available, car ownership isn't essential, though most residents choose to have one for the flexibility it provides.

Is Rio Real safe?

Yes. Rio Real is considered one of the more secure residential areas in Marbella. The communities are quiet, well-lit and either gated or privately managed. Crime rates in this part of Marbella are low by the standards of European cities, and residents consistently describe feeling safe walking, cycling and going about daily life. As with any residential area anywhere in the world, common sense applies, but security concerns don't rank highly among the things that people who've moved here mention.

What property types are available in Rio Real?

One of Rio Real's genuine strengths is the variety. You'll find luxury villas with private pools and panoramic sea views, contemporary detached homes in modern communities, townhouses in small and well-managed urbanisations, garden and golf apartments, frontline beach properties, and elevated penthouses with terrace views that span the whole coastline. The range means the area attracts buyers at several different price points, and there's usually something available across different property types, though stock is always limited relative to demand for the area's most desirable positions.

How far is Málaga Airport from Rio Real?

Around 35 to 40 minutes by car in normal traffic conditions, using the AP-7 motorway. This makes Rio Real one of the more accessible parts of Marbella for international buyers and for residents who travel frequently. It's close enough to make airport runs straightforward, without being so close that flight paths become an issue, the flight corridors over Málaga Airport run in a different direction, so noise is not a factor in Rio Real.

Are there restaurants near Rio Real?

Yes, and the range is better than the residential setting might suggest. Rio Real Golf has its own well-regarded restaurant with a terrace overlooking the course and, beyond it, the sea. Trocadero, a short drive towards Marbella East, is one of the most celebrated beachfront dining spots in the whole of Marbella. The Point Marbella offers a younger, beach-club style setting. And Marbella's old town, with its extraordinary density of restaurants across every price point and style, is five minutes away. Most residents would say the restaurant situation is one of the area's underrated strengths.

Can dogs use the beaches near Rio Real?

Yes. Marbella's designated dog beach is a short drive east from Rio Real and is genuinely well-used, early mornings there have a particular community feel, with dog owners from across the area converging for the off-lead stretch by the sea. The Senda Litoral is also excellent for dogs, and the quieter sections east of Rio Real are particularly good for morning walks. For residents with dogs, the area works well.

Why choose Rio Real over Los Monteros?

Both are excellent residential areas and there's a fair amount of overlap in who they suit. Los Monteros is particularly known for its frontline beach position and the landmark Los Monteros hotel, if being as close to the sea as possible is the priority, it's a strong choice. Rio Real tends to offer more variety in property type and a wider price range, and the golf course setting gives it a different character, more green corridors, more elevated positions with views, more residential variety. Many buyers look seriously at both before deciding. The best choice depends on which combination of factors matters most to you.

Is Rio Real expensive compared to the rest of Marbella?

Rio Real sits broadly in the mid-to-upper range for Marbella property, above the more budget-conscious parts of Marbella East but below the premium pricing of the Golden Mile, Sierra Blanca or parts of Los Monteros frontline. For what the area offers, location, views, community quality, school access, beach proximity, many buyers find it represents genuine value relative to the most expensive Marbella postcodes. The price range is also wide: a well-positioned villa can command a significant price, while a good apartment in one of the established communities can offer considerably better value.

Can I cycle into Marbella from Rio Real?

Yes, and many residents do. The Senda Litoral is suitable for cyclists and the route into Marbella is mostly flat, following the coastline. The journey takes around 20 to 30 minutes by bike, long enough to feel like proper exercise, short enough to be genuinely practical for a morning commute or a trip into town. Several residents cycle it daily, particularly in the cooler months. In summer, the heat makes early morning or evening trips more comfortable.

Is there public transport from Rio Real?

Yes, though the area is primarily car-oriented. Bus services connect parts of Rio Real and Marbella East to Marbella centre and beyond, running reasonably frequently during the day. For local trips and the journey into Marbella, many residents without a car use a combination of bus, cycling on the Senda Litoral and taxis or ride-sharing services. It's manageable, but a car makes life more flexible and convenient, and most residents choose to have one.

Why do Rio Real residents choose to stay?

Ask anyone who's lived here for more than a few years and you'll hear variations on the same answer: the balance. The peace, the green surroundings, the views, the fact that you can reach Marbella in five minutes and the airport in 35. The sense that the area has genuine character rather than being assembled for the property market. The feeling of knowing your neighbours and the streets and the best time to walk the Senda Litoral. And, for many, the October light, when the summer has passed and the area quietens back into itself and reminds you why you chose it in the first place.
